# Filter Media Comparison

How the four common filter-media types — **cellulose paper**, **synthetic fibre**,
**blended**, and **nano-fibre** — compare on structure, efficiency, dust capacity,
pressure drop, service life, cost and best-fit applications.

!!! note "Source & status"
    This page is **framework reference + editorial**, not a per-SKU specification. The
    typical figures below are industry-common reference ranges (`[框架参考]`), not
    measured values for any specific huiying SKU. Subjective conclusions are signed
    (`小筑 ✍️`) and are procurement guidance, not product claims.

## Comparison matrix

| Attribute | Cellulose paper | Blended (cellulose + synthetic) | Synthetic fibre | Nano-fibre |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Structure | Resin-impregnated wood-pulp fibre sheet | Mixed cellulose / synthetic layers | Non-woven polymer fibres (e.g. PET/PP) | Sub-micron electrospun layer on a substrate |
| Filtration efficiency (typical) | ~95–99% (coarser, `[框架参考]`) | ~98–99% | ~99–99.5% | ≥99.5% (sub-micron capture) |
| Dust-holding capacity | Lower | Medium | Higher | Highest per media area |
| Pressure drop | Lower (open pores) | Medium | Medium–higher | Higher (fine pores need more area) |
| Service life | Shorter (moisture-sensitive) | Medium | Longer (moisture-tolerant) | Longest (deep-loading) |
| Relative cost | Lowest | Low–medium | Medium–high | Highest |
| Best-fit scenario | Price-sensitive, normal-duty | Balanced default | High-dust, heavy-duty, humid | Premium / long-interval / high-efficiency |

## Micro characteristics by media type

### Cellulose paper `[行业常识]`
Natural wood-pulp fibre, resin-impregnated for wet strength and pleat rigidity. Fibres are
relatively coarse and the pore structure is irregular, so efficiency is inherently lower
and moisture can degrade the sheet. Its strength is **cost** and adequate protection in
normal conditions.

### Blended (cellulose + synthetic) `[行业常识]`
A mix of cellulose with a synthetic fibre layer improves efficiency, wet strength and
dust loading while keeping cost moderate. It is the common middle ground for aftermarket
air and oil filters where the balance of price and performance matters most.

### Synthetic fibre `[行业常识]`
Non-woven polymer media with finer, more uniform fibres. Finer fibres give higher
efficiency and dust capacity, and the material resists moisture better than cellulose.
The trade-off is cost and often a slightly higher pressure drop unless the media area is
increased.

### Nano-fibre `[框架参考]`
An ultra-fine electrospun layer (sub-micron fibres) applied over a substrate forms a dense
surface layer that captures very fine particles while the open substrate keeps flow up.
It delivers the highest efficiency and dust loading, aimed at premium, long-interval or
high-filtration applications.

## Buying advice `小筑 ✍️`

- **High-dust markets** (construction, agriculture, desert, unpaved roads): prioritise
  **synthetic or blended** media — dust-holding capacity and moisture tolerance protect
  the engine longer between services, which is worth more than the unit-price saving.
- **Cost-sensitive markets**: **cellulose** is a legitimate, proven choice where dust load
  is normal and service intervals are short; do not pay a premium for media the duty cycle
  does not need.
- **Premium / long-interval positioning**: offer **nano-fibre** as the "good-better-best"
  top tier — sell it on total cost of ownership (fewer changes, better protection), not on
  media price alone.
- **Whichever media you choose, match it by OE**: media grade does not compensate for a
  wrong panel dimension, seal or bypass-valve specification. Confirm the exact part before
  ordering — see [Fitment Reference](../fitment/index.md).
